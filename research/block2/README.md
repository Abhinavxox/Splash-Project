# Block 2. How blind and low vision people study now

First hand and published accounts, collected 2026-11-11. 55 sources, each opened.

- `01-blind-scientists.md` 15 accounts of blind scientists (Nemeth, Cullers, Minkara, Wedler, Diaz-Merced, Vermeij and others)
- `02-blind-students.md` 11 accounts of blind and low vision university students, 3 Canadian
- `03-community-posts.md` 16 posts from NFB mailing lists and Hacker News
- `04-studies.md` 13 studies that interviewed or surveyed these students, 2005 to 2024

## What the four sets agree on

1. Math, graphs, diagrams and board work are the hardest content. Math in PDFs or images does not read and diagrams are lost without a description or tactile version. All four sets.
2. Material arrives late, in the wrong format or not at all. Having an accommodation on paper is not the same as getting access. All four sets.
3. The gap is filled by people. Readers, assistants, tutors, friends and the disability office. All four sets.
4. The student carries the extra work and time. Self advocacy, making their own formats, weekends fixing PDFs, much longer to get through a video. Students, posts, studies.
5. Instructors and advisors often do not know how to help, and attitudes sometimes block people more than materials. Scientists, students, studies.
6. Preparing early works. Lessons sent ahead, source files like LaTeX or Word instead of PDF. Scientists, posts.

## Through the six questions

| Question | What the evidence says so far |
|---|---|
| For whom | Blind and low vision university students, especially in math heavy and STEM courses |
| Objectives | Follow lectures, study the same material, complete assignments and exams on time like classmates |
| Difficulties | Visual content (math, graphs, diagrams, image only slides) unreadable, material late or wrong format, live board work not described |
| Why | Material made visually first and fixed later. Fixing depends on people and offices that are short of time or STEM experience. Instructors unaware. Instructors share PDFs rather than source files |
| Consequences | Hours of extra work, falling behind, dependence on others, some moving out of their chosen field |
| How | Not decided. Needs local evidence first |

## Goes against

- Many blind people do succeed in STEM with the right people and tools. Some say plain text or LaTeX works well and human description is rarely needed.
- Several say attitudes and prejudice are now a bigger barrier than technology.
- Some institutions are aware and working on it.
- Survivorship bias. The scientists all succeeded, so the difficulty is probably understated for those who left.

## Gaps
