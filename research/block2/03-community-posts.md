### Helping blind students with algebra/statistics at a community college
https://nfbnet.org/pipermail/nfbcs_nfbnet.org/2017-January/010435.html
type: mailing list (NFBCS)
who: blind alternate-media specialist at a community college + blind PhD student/engineer reply (2017-01-11 to 01-15)
goal: help blind students pass pre-algebra, algebra, statistics
difficulty: instructor works equations on the board, points and speaks in fragments; no app lets student work like pencil and paper; whiteboard magnification does not work for low vision students
cause: math taught visually; tutors do not know how to teach someone who cannot see; poor earlier schooling (per reply)
consequence: failing intermediate algebra can block an associate degree; failing stats blocks most humanities degrees
workaround: get information ahead of time; braille/Nemeth; Bookshare, Learning Ally; notes on iDevices with screen reader or Zoom
notes: "Getting the information ahead of time is the most useful technique" (reply). Original asks "How has math become such a highly visual subject to teach?"

## patterns
- Math and graphs are the hard part: math in PDFs/images does not read; graphs and diagrams are lost without tactile output or description (many posts).
- Materials arrive in the wrong format or late, and the fix is a human (reader, disability office, tutor, champion); getting material ahead of time is repeatedly the top tip.
- The student usually ends up doing the self-advocacy and tool research; staff and counselors are described as inexperienced with blind STEM students.
- Source formats matter: LaTeX/MathML/accessible HTML or Word readable; PDFs, scans and slide images are not. Several posts say send source files, not just PDFs.
- Live-lecture issues: board work and pointing/"this" (instructor speaks in fragments) and low vision students find magnifying whiteboards does not work.
- No one in this set described PowerPoint slide structure (reading order, alt text) in their own words; that gap is real in what I could open (Reddit/AppleVis blocked).

## goes against
- Several posters say blind people do succeed in math/CS with the right people and tools ("there is definitely a way"; mentor says do not drop out).
- Posters recommend LaTeX/plain text as workable and even preferable, e.g. a blind friend read LaTeX source on a braille display.
- A blind commenter says human description is needed only "in the rare case" (HN 2016 thread).
- Tools called "really easy" when hardware is available (embossing graphs from R).
- Note: these are mostly older (2011-2017) list posts, so tools may have improved.

## summary
- 16 opened posts: 13 NFB mailing list threads (BlindMath/NABS-L/NFBCS), 3 Hacker News threads; Reddit and AppleVis could not be opened.
- Main blockers: math/graphs inaccessible in PDF/image form, late or wrong-format materials, and reliance on readers/offices that lack experience.
- Common workarounds: ask for materials ahead of time, LaTeX/MathML, tactile graphics, hired readers; slide-specific first-person evidence is thin.
