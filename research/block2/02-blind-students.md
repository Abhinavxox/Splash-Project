# Block 2: blind / low vision students and lecture material
accessed 2026-11-11. Rough notes. Only pages I actually opened. Several pages returned 403 (NPR main, Educause, NFB guidelines, Stanford news, Benetech); not used.

### How a Blind Student Who Felt Locked Out of STEM Classes Challenged and Changed Her University (EdSurge)
https://edsurge.com/news/2017-12-08-how-a-blind-student-who-felt-locked-out-of-stem-classes-challenged-and-changed-her-university
type: news
who: Emily Schlenker, blind pre-med student, Wichita State (US)
goal: do organic chemistry / pre-med coursework with same access as sighted peers
difficulty: homework charts and graphs unreadable by screen reader; math courseware (ALEKS) had pictures; online science textbook graphics carried key info; materials not usable in time
cause: materials not designed accessibly; instructors assumed commercial products were accessible; publishers not bound like colleges; some profs resisted changing materials (academic freedom)
consequence: filed complaint with Dept of Education OCR with NFB help; won settlement (same-time delivery of materials, tech audit, access coordinator, grievance process)
workaround: screen reader for text; some professors became allies
notes: "you can compel certain changes, but you can't compel a cultural change." Article says blind students rarely major in math/science.

### Publishers, universities struggle to provide timely access to accessible textbooks (University Affairs)
https://universityaffairs.ca/news/publishers-universities-struggle-to-provide-timely-access-to-accessible-textbooks/
type: news (Canada)
who: Alycia Pottie, legally blind psychology student, Mount Saint Vincent U; Didier Chelin, McGill law student
goal: get accessible e-textbooks at start of term; Chelin also wanted a scribe for exams/assignments
difficulty: Pottie waited 1-2+ weeks for books in most courses; Chelin went months without accessible materials and no scribe
cause: publishers want proof of purchase before releasing accessible texts; accessibility services understaffed; prof handouts/articles often inaccessible; rules vary by province
consequence: Pottie did weeks of catch-up, felt set up to fail; Chelin's complaint to Quebec Human Rights Commission still pending, could take years
workaround: bought books early, sent proof of payment via accessibility office, self-advocated; Chelin filed formal complaint
notes: "There isn't enough staffing in accessibility services to keep on top of publishers." Textbooks tied to publisher platforms = multiple logins/layouts.

### Breaking Barriers for Visually Impaired Students (University of Alberta, Centre for Teaching and Learning)
https://www.ualberta.ca/en/centre-for-teaching-and-learning/news/2026/04/breaking-barriers-for-visually-impaired-students.html
type: profile (Canada, university news)
who: Aarush Bhat (2nd-yr computing science, light perception only); Caden Johnson (1st-yr CS, low vision); plus their math tutor/accessibility coach
goal: succeed in STEM at U of A
difficulty: lectures relying on pointing at board; slides with images of text/code; R, R Commander, SPSS lack accessibility; web systems with dropdowns not usable with AT
cause: assumptions blind people can't do STEM; institutions slow to admit systems are flawed; vendors not tested with real users; accessibility bolted on
consequence: Caden moved from Engineering to Computer Science to find less visual courses; Aarush was barred from advanced STEM in India; tutors/staff absorb accommodation work
workaround: BrailleNote Touch, Be My Eyes, smart glasses; Aarush mixes ASCII Math/LaTeX/Markdown, wrote Python wrapper for inaccessible emulator; some profs read board work aloud; escalated to admin
notes: Caden says he "lose[s] everything" when instructor points at the board. Switch-out of Engineering = relevant to leaving/slowing STEM.

### Why 5-Minute Video Lectures Took Me 30 Minutes as a Blind Student (Access Interrupted, Substack)
https://accessinterrupted.substack.com/p/why-5-minute-video-lectures-took
type: blog
who: Maria Reyes (name as given on the page), blind college student, BrailleSense 6 Mini; I could not confirm her identity or school elsewhere
goal: learn from video lectures in an AWS Cloud Foundations course
difficulty: pausing every minute to type terms in braille, rewinding; short videos ballooned
cause: listening, typing, remembering, rewinding all at once; attention on note-taking mechanics
consequence: exhausting, less enjoyable, drained energy
workaround: recorded the audio with Just Press Record, fed to Google NotebookLM for summaries/questions, audio overviews, still verified outputs
notes: "A five minute lecture video could easily turn into thirty minutes or more." Blind students "spend more energy on access than on understanding."

### Conversations about accessible education (University of Calgary news, research by a master's student)
https://ucalgary.ca/news/conversations-accessible-education
type: profile / research summary (Canada); students are anonymous participants
who: students with vision loss interviewed by a grad researcher
goal: participate fully in classes
difficulty: textbooks and research articles not compatible with their tools; instructors reactive not proactive; one student could not follow when lights dimmed for presentations while content was on the board
cause: instructors had little knowledge of vision loss; attitudes and assumptions
consequence: some accommodations ignored; being listed in documentation did not guarantee participation
workaround: built-in phone accessibility features (discreet), text-to-speech, self-advocacy, collaborating with instructors/accessibility services
notes: secondhand summary of interviews, not direct quotes from students. "reactionary, rather than proactive."

### Study tips for low vision students (Veroniiiica blog)
https://veroniiiica.com/2017/03/09/study-tips-for-low-vision-students/
type: blog
who: Veronica Lewis, college student with low vision and print disability
goal: study effectively with accessible formats
difficulty: standard print impossible; accommodated large font tiring; white backgrounds make lines "collide"; math problems and code hard to read character by character; eye strain, headaches
cause: visual load of print/screens
consequence: fatigue, needs breaks
workaround: magnification, dark mode/tints/line guides, request accessible slides/notes/code, recordings with transcripts, TTS/audio, braille/tactile, dry-erase board for working problems
notes: low vision not blind; mostly workaround list. Math and code specifically named as hardest.

### These blind students say their college blocked their education (NPR via KPBS / NHPR)
https://www.kpbs.org/news/education/2026/04/06/these-blind-students-say-their-college-blocked-their-education-a-new-rule-could-help
(also read https://www.nhpr.org/2026-04-06/these-blind-students-say-their-college-blocked-their-education-a-new-rule-could-help)
type: news
who: Harold Rogers and Miranda Lacy, blind grad students, WVU online Master of Social Work (US)
goal: finish the MSW to become psychotherapist / social worker
difficulty: PDFs and readings unreadable by screen reader; dozens of inaccessible docs per semester; unlabeled charts/images
cause: inaccessible digital materials; slow university response; ADA never gave technical web standards
consequence: more time troubleshooting than learning, lost weekends; Rogers says faced discipline after requesting accommodations; lawsuit with NFB
workaround: Lacy learned screen readers/VoiceOver/smart speakers before starting; the two help each other; repeated requests before suing
notes: "going down a ski slope without any assistance." Screen reader says "Unlabeled text box, unlabeled image, page break." Not STEM but same material types.

### Evening the odds (Wake Forest news)

### Making engineering education accessible to blind and low-vision learners (Stanford Engineering)
https://engineering.stanford.edu/news/making-engineering-education-accessible-blind-and-low-vision-learners
type: profile / research news
who: Trisha Kulkarni, blind student in intro electrical engineering course ENGR 40M
goal: complete the lab-based course
difficulty: soldering, tracking circuit layout, simulators showing "waves and curves" screen readers can't convey, juggling three mental models of a transistor, one inaccessible program
cause: course designed visually; resources not found in time
consequence: researchers found hardest parts "often came from a point of inequity"; she said of soldering resources "I did not have time to learn."
workaround: instructors drew on her palm; talking through concepts beat braille printouts; alternative software swapped in
notes: she finished the course. Researchers recommend alt text and verbal slide descriptions.

### Students sue over inaccessible course tech (Learning/legal summary, LFL)
https://www.lflegal.com/2023/05/blind-college-students-jury-verdict/
type: news / legal summary (second hand; no direct student voice beyond one paraphrase)
who: Roy Payan and Portia Mason, blind students, Los Angeles Community College District, with NFB
goal: use required course software and texts
difficulty: MyMathLab and math and other course tools unusable with screen reader; textbooks, sites, databases not accessible
cause: district did not supply accessible tools and content
consequence: jury award ($240,000 per article), later cut then reportedly reinstated on appeal; damages described as loss of educational opportunity, grade damage, delayed degrees
workaround: asked for accessible tools before suing (no result); lawsuit
notes: Payan says professor told him blind students "weren't welcome in his class" (as paraphrased by article).

## patterns
- Images with no text (slide screenshots of code, charts/graphs, courseware pictures, unlabeled PDF elements) were the most repeated barrier: Alberta, Schlenker, WVU, LACCD, Stanford sims.
- Late or missing accessible materials, caused by publisher rules, understaffed disability offices, or no one owning the job (Pottie, Chelin, Schlenker). Students catch up late or complain formally.
- Extra time cost for the blind student: 12 hrs/wk stats, 30 min for 5 min video, weekends fixing PDFs. Work shifts to the student or tutors.
- Workarounds: braille notetakers, LaTeX/ASCII math, embossed graphs, recording audio plus AI summary, Be My Eyes, reading board aloud, peers/tutors, self-built scripts. Many are self-made.
- Staff/professors often do not know how to help (Stimmel, Calgary, Chelin). Leaving or shifting: Caden Engineering to CS; Alberta student barred from advanced STEM in India.

## goes against
- Wake Forest (Webster) and Stanford (Kulkarni) succeeded in math/stats and EE; Schlenker got fixes after complaint. But each needed big extra effort or special help.
- Calgary: mindful instructors had a "considerable positive impact"; some Alberta profs read board work aloud; phone built-ins were convenient.
- Veroniiiica is low vision and mostly lists workable fixes.
- No YouTube source included: video pages I opened (e.g. "Episode 2 - Choosing a University and Picking a Course as a Blind or Partially Sighted Student", "Accessible Coding for Students using a Screen Reader") showed no description or transcript, so I could not use them. Not found: CBC or NEADS/RNIB student testimony. No firsthand source of a student who fully quit STEM; Caden's switch is the nearest.
- Texas A&M piece had a prof saying "we do not have students who are blind because mathematics is not accessible" but no student account; not included as source. Benetech (high school student) opened but not university, excluded.
