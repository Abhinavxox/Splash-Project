What a screen reader gets from a real lecture PDF

A real, publicly available lecture: Stanford CS231n, Lecture 2. Each slide below shows what a sighted student sees, what a screen reader can actually get out of the file, and what it should have said.

Slide 23 · Distance metric

What the screen reader gets

  1. Distance Metric to compare images.
  2. L1 distance:
  3. add
  4. Fei-Fei Li, Ehsan Adeli. Lecture 2 - 23. April 4, 2023.

What is lost

  • The formula d₁(I₁, I₂) = Σ |I₁ᵖ − I₂ᵖ| is a picture, so it is silent.
  • All three 4×4 tables (48 numbers) are pictures, so they are silent.
  • The answer, 456, is never read.
  • Only the word “add” survives, with nothing around it.

What it should say

  • Distance metric to compare images. L1 distance: d sub 1 of I 1, I 2 equals the sum over pixels p of the absolute value of I 1 p minus I 2 p.
  • Worked example, a table with 3 grids of 4 by 4 pixels: test image minus training image gives pixel-wise absolute differences. Example, top-left: 56 minus 10 is 46.
  • Adding all 16 differences gives a distance of 456.

Slide 53 · Linear classifier

What the screen reader gets

  1. Parametric Approach: Linear Classifier.
  2. 3072x1
  3. Image
  4. f(x,W) = W x + b 10x1
  5. 10x1
  6. 10x3072
  7. f(x,W)
  8. Array of 32x32x3 numbers (3072 numbers total)
  9. 10 numbers giving class scores
  10. W
  11. parameters or weights

What is lost

  • Matrix sizes are read in a jumbled order and attached to nothing: “b 10x1, 10x1, 10x3072”.
  • The colours that link each size to its term (W is red, x is blue) are lost.
  • The arrows (image goes into f, W feeds f, out come 10 scores) are lost.
  • The cat photo is silent.

What it should say

  • Linear classifier: f of x, W equals W times x plus b.
  • Sizes: x is the image flattened to 3072 by 1. W, the weights, is 10 by 3072. b is 10 by 1. The output f is 10 by 1.
  • Flow: an input image, a cat of 32 by 32 by 3 numbers, goes into f together with the learned weights W, and comes out as 10 class scores.

Slide 22 · Nearest neighbour

What the screen reader gets

  1. First classifier: Nearest Neighbor.
  2. ?
  3. deer
  4. bird
  5. plane
  6. cat
  7. car
  8. Training data with labels
  9. query data
  10. Distance Metric
  11. Fei-Fei Li, Ehsan Adeli. Lecture 2 - 22. April 4, 2023.

What is lost

  • Six photos are silent, so “deer bird plane cat car” is just a list of words.
  • The question mark is read before the labels, out of place.
  • The key idea is gone: compare the query cat against each training image to get one number.

What it should say

  • Nearest neighbour classifier. Training data: 5 small labelled photos, a deer, a bird, a plane, a cat and a car.
  • Query: a new photo of a cat whose label is unknown, marked with a question mark.
  • A distance metric takes two images, here the query cat and the training cat, and returns one real number. The closest training image gives the label.

The problem in one line

The PDF stores text boxes and pictures, but not their meaning. Formulas, tables and diagrams are saved as images, so the screen reader has nothing to say. What text there is comes out in layout order rather than reading order.

The device and the screen reader are not the issue. The file is missing the meaning, and today a person has to add it back by hand.

How this was made: the “gets” column is the text pulled from the PDF in its stored order, which is what a screen reader receives from an untagged file. NVDA may also say “graphic” for some images or vary the order slightly. Confirm with a live NVDA run on Windows before using these as final evidence.
