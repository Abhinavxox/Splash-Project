## What the course actually asks for

**Goal.** "Your goal is not to prove that you can build software. Your goal is to demonstrate that you can reason and act as a software engineer." But a product is still required.

**Mission (overview).** Build a software intervention for real users with a real, documented problem. By the final defense, show that the problem, as defined within your declared scope, "has disappeared or been reduced to the success threshold you established in the real target context." Also present a strategy for scaling that effect.

**A working release, not a prototype.** The release must be "operationally complete within the scope you claim," and use real data and conditions. "A prototype can be useful during the project, but your final claims must rely on operation with the real data." Narrow scope is fine: "Reduce the scope if necessary, but demonstrate what you claim."

**Six responsibilities, handled together:** build a software product; define the method of investigation and engineering; control resources and costs, including the value of each person's hours; solve a real problem; demonstrate impact (criteria, baseline, threshold, all defined *before* results); assure quality, with your own rubrics compared against the teaching team's.

**What to show at the final defense (Dec 2–9), seven points:**
1. The problem is real.
2. The intervention is appropriate and feasible.
3. The product works, shown live.
4. The method protected quality while managing resources.
5. The solution removes the problem, with observed evidence.
6. The impact is real and scalable.
7. Everything is evidence-based.

No business model.

**Grading.** The milestones count 0% and only set the Competency Floor. Final grade: product quality 15% (group), methodology 15% (group), reflection 10% (individual reflective reports). There are no marks for effort, lines of code or number of features.

**Each milestone needs** a team Progress Report and an *individual reflective report* from each student, showing their reasoning: beliefs, evidence, alternatives, what they expected and what happened.

## Gaps for our team, as of Nov 25

- The impact criteria, baseline and threshold were never defined in advance. They are required for points 5 and 6.
- No working release: `prototype/` covers 3 slides with hand-written descriptions.
- No real users have tested it in the target context.
- No resource or cost tracking (people's hours, licences, API costs).
- No team rubrics or comparison with the teaching team's rubrics.
- Individual reflective reports: check they exist for each milestone.
