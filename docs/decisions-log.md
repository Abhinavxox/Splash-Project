# Decisions log

## October 7. Move away from the GPU scaling project

Decision. Stop working on the GPU scaling plan for a fictional startup.
Options. Keep it with a narrower scope and a real stakeholder, or find a new problem.
Evidence. Feedback from the professor and GA that we could not show real evidence, the scope mixed business, DevOps and model optimization, and optimization was too big for the time left.
Would change if. We found a real team with real traffic willing to work with us.

## October 10 to 18. Look at other problems during reading week

Decision. Compare other ideas before committing.
Options. A narrower GPU project, border wait times, bus arrival predictions, flaky tests, a food bank system, accessible course material.
Evidence. Most ideas were already solved by existing apps such as Google Maps, the Transit app and Umo.

## October 24 and 25. Work on accessible lecture slides

Decision. Focus on lecture slides that screen reader users cannot understand.
Evidence. A real Stanford lecture gave a screen reader almost nothing from formula, table and diagram slides. Existing tools solve pieces separately and mostly for papers or charts.
Would change if. Adobe or other tools turn out to handle slides well in our own test.

## October 25. Do not build braille output

Decision. Leave braille translation to existing tools.
Evidence. Liblouis and MathCAT already produce braille from structured content.

## October 25. Use HTML as the main output instead of tagged PDF

Decision. Produce accessible HTML first.
Evidence. When our prototype exported a tagged PDF, headings and tables kept their tags but formulas lost their meaning.
Would change if. The accessibility office requires PDF and we find a way to keep math tags.
