**Our problem statement today**

Blind and low vision university students must learn from lecture material made for sighted students. We do not yet know what actually stops them, why, or what it costs them.

Right now this is a hypothesis, not a proven problem. Published evidence points to it, but nobody local has confirmed it. Today's job is to decide, as a team, whether it is valid enough to keep investigating and which hypotheses we test.

**The hypotheses to test**

| # | Hypothesis | Evidence so far |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Math, graphs, diagrams and image only slides are unreadable with screen readers and braille displays | Strong. Our PDF scan, studies, students, scientists and posts |
| H2 | Accessible material reaches students too late to keep up | Mixed. Students report delays, published turnaround is 2 to 10 business days |
| H3 | Making material accessible costs staff and instructors a lot of manual time | Some. Job postings and tool reviews, no measured hours |
| H4 | Instructors do not know how or that they need to make material accessible | Repeated in studies and student accounts |
| H5 | Instructors share PDFs when the original file would work better | Some posts |
| H6 | Students' screen readers, braille displays and devices fail on the material as it is shared | Partly. Tools work on structured content, fail on images and untagged PDFs |
| H7 | The SAS request process itself causes the delay | Unknown |
| H8 | Live lectures, board work and pointing, are a bigger barrier than files | Some posts and student accounts |
| H9 | Attitudes and low expectations block students more than material | Some scientists say so |
| H10 | Students spend a lot of extra time because of this | A few examples |
| H11 | It is not a real problem here because SAS already handles it well | Unknown. This is the null hypothesis, we must test it honestly |

These match questions A to K in `research/research-questions.md`.

**The process we follow**

We use an iterative, risk driven lifecycle, in the style of the spiral model. We chose it because our biggest risk is not technical. It is that the problem is not what we think. A risk driven lifecycle deals with the biggest risk first and only moves on when it is reduced. Waterfall would fix requirements before we know the problem. Full Scrum assumes a known product backlog, which we do not have yet.

| Phase | Dates | What we do | Exit criterion |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Problem validation | Nov 13 to 17 | Hypotheses, reading, SAS, students, instructors | Each hypothesis marked supported, rejected or unknown. Team verdict recorded |
| 2 Requirements and scope | Nov 18 to 19 | User needs, requirements traced to needs, scope, success measures with baselines | Requirements agreed and traced to evidence |
| 3 Design and prototypes | Nov 20 to 23 | Two or three prototypes, each tied to a confirmed cause | Each prototype runs on real course material |
| 4 Evaluation | Nov 24 to 25 | Test against success measures, before and after | Results table complete |
| 5 Defense | Nov 26 | Report, slides, demo | Each member presents their part |

At the end of each phase the team reviews the evidence and records a decision. If evidence rejects a hypothesis we change direction and record why. That is not a failure. It is the process working.
