Title: Paper 1
Generative AI for Software Project Management: Insights from a Review of a Software
Practitioner Literature

Authors, year, where published:
Lakshana Iruni Assalaarachchi (Monash University)
Zainab Masood (Prince Sultan University)
Rasgina Hoda (Monash University)
John Grundy (Monash University)

Year: 2025
2025 IEEE “accepted on 7th October 2025”

Published in: IEEE Software, a magazine/journal

Type of Study:
Grey Literature Review

Who They Studied?
They studied that software project managers mainly use GenAI as their “assistant” or a
“friend” rather than their replacement. They use GenAI for communication, collaboration,
performing repetitive tasks and predictive analysis for the project’s success.

Main Finding
The study found out that GenAI has started to show its ability to transform the software
engineering field. However, for an SPM role, it is evolving alongside GenAI and is being
used to perform routine activities. Moreover, the major concern faced by the authors is how
GenAI is being used by software practitioners in an SPM role and the challenges faced by
them while using the AI.

Engineer vs Manager:
Both roles need communication and collaboration, so for that ultimately managers are
needed in both of them.

Useful for us because:
This paper gives us the evidence of how SPM use GenAI in their work and the benefits they
get from using the AI.
This paper also gives us an insight into the concerns of the software engineers and
managers regarding the use of AI for their work.

Connection with other papers:
Agrees with other papers regarding success factors that influence agile software
development project success (Nguyen), success factors as critical that shape agile projects'
success (Nguyen), excellence practices in Scrum (Pashchenko, 2024), reliable estimates
and Software Development, Jordanian Enterprises (2018), and Crowdsourcing in Software
Development (Bibi et al., 2020).

Interview Question Idea
    ●​ Do you agree with the paper that says practitioners use GenAI as their assistant?
    ●​ Do you use AI for confidential projects as well? Where do you draw the line?
    ●​ Do you rely on the use of AI for making an important decision about the project
       development, delivery and execution?
    ●​ Can you share an incident where GenAI truly saved you from making a huge blunder
       on a project?

Title: Paper 2
Success Factors that Influence Agile Software Development Project Success

Author:
Dan Schilling Nguyen, Ph.D., Mt Washington College National Online Program, Alpharetta,
Georgia, USA (single author).

Year: 2016

Published in: American Scientific Research Journal for Engineering, Technology, and
Sciences (ASRJETS), Volume 17, No. 1, pp. 172–222.

Type of Study:
Qualitative and phenomenological study

Who They Studied?
They studied the lived experiences of 10 agile software development team project managers
and leaders from global companies in the United States.

Main Finding
The study found out that for the agile software projects to succeed, there need to be few of
the things going in the right direction. According to the insights given by 10 agile managers,
during the software development process, customers prefer to stay engaged in the whole
process. This basically helps for the successful completion of the project due to the team's
scrum-style process. Moreover, an effective product owner maximises the business value,
sets priorities, engages stakeholders and follows good agile engineering practices for their
software to perform properly.

Engineer vs Manager:
The technical side of the paper is described from the managers' point of view because only
they were interviewed. As both the technical and managerial side both are equally important,
they are interlinked.

Useful for us because:
This paper shows the gap in engineers' opinions, which will be filled once we interview them
but for now only managers' views regarding success of agile projects were noted.
Connection with other papers
Connections to other papers such as Nguyen's companion paper (2016), the GenAI paper
(2025), and the Scrum paper (Pashchenko, 2024)

Quote or number worth citing
“9/10 (90%) participants believe good agile engineering practices are key to the project's
success. The paper also cites 2012 data of 72% success for agile vs 64% for traditional
methods.

Interview Question Idea
    ●​ What is the most important factor for the project development, the technical aspects
       or the managerial aspects?

Title: Paper 3

Success Factors as Critical That Shape Agile Software Development Project Success

Author: Dan Schilling Nguyen

Year: 2016

Where: International Journal of Sciences: Basic and Applied Research (IJSBAR), Vol. 25,
No. 3, pp. 20–69.

Type of study: Interviews.

A qualitative, phenomenological study: open-ended questions answered by email, then
coded into themes.

Who they studied: The paper studied 10 agile software development managers or leaders
in the United States (5 in IT, 2 in manufacturing and 3 in defence). None of the engineers
were interviewed or studied.

Main Finding
The study found out that the project will only succeed if the team communicates well with
every layer of the hierarchy of the company and if engineering practices are closely
implemented and monitored, the product owner maximises the product value and also if the
sponsors fund the project.

Engineer vs Manager:
The technical side of the paper is described from the managers' point of view because only
they were interviewed. As both the technical and managerial side both are equally important,
they are interlinked.


Useful for us because:
This paper shows the gap in engineers' opinions, which will be filled once we interview them,
but for now only managers' views regarding the success of agile projects were noted.
Connection with other papers
Connections to other papers such as Nguyen's companion paper (2016), the GenAI paper
(2025), and the Scrum paper (Pashchenko, 2024).

Quote or number worth citing
“9/10 (90%) participants believe that automation helps for continuous integration of agile
development principles, and design patterns help to reduce technical debt as well. The same
majority pointed out that collaboration and communication skills are core success factors of
a project.

Interview question idea: “Is continuous integration of development principles more
significant than the communication skills of the team itself?”

Title: Paper 4

Reliable Estimate and Software Development/Jordanian Environment

Authors: Mohammad Mahmoud Tarawneh and Sattam Rakan Allahawiah (Balqa Applied
University / Karak University College)

Year: May 2018

Where: International Journal of Computer Applications, Vol. 179, No. 44, pp. 10–14.

Type of study: Survey (questionnaire) with follow-up interviews.

Who they studied: The paper studied software project managers in Jordan. Questionnaires
were given to 120 managers at a software company, and 20 of the managers were
interviewed.

Main Finding
The study found out that the managers in Jordan repeatedly made the same mistakes while
developing a project without learning from them. Furthermore, they could not allocate proper
time and budget for a project to succeed, due to which they would always rely on past
experience without adapting to modern tools and technologies. Due to this issue, the project
always exceeded the timeline and budget allocation

Engineer vs Manager:
The paper focuses entirely on the manager's inability to perform well from their side during
the project development process.

Useful for us because:
This paper shows how managers can also often struggle with their work, such as the
estimation of numbers and their core managerial tasks.

Connection with other papers
The GenAI paper (2025) and The Nguyen papers (2016)
Quote or number worth citing
The usability of the system is affected by 59.70% when a project manager fails to estimate
its cost.
Interview question idea: what kind of tools and technologies do you use to estimate the
cost and timeline for a project?
What do you keep in mind to deliver the project on time?


Title: Paper 5

Excellence Practices in the Scrum Paradigm in Software Development

Author: Denis Pashchenko (SlavaSoft, Spain)

Year: 2025 (online Sept 2024)

Where: Journal of Data Science and Intelligent Systems, Vol. 3, No. 2, pp. 79–86

Type of study: Review

Who they studied: The paper is based on the author's industry experience and research
from 2020 to 2023 across Russian, European and international IT companies. So, there are
no participants.

Main Finding
The study found out that scrum practices work well when the goals are SMART and easily
measurable. It also goes well when all the team roles are combined with AI tools to save
time from doing repetitive tasks. Apart from this remote work, and more digitalisation makes
scrum rely more on communication and documentation.

Engineer vs Manager:
The paper focuses on both roles but from a manager's point of view.

Useful for us because:
This paper shows how work is divided among managers and engineers in an scrum team.

Connection with other papers
The paper is closest to the GenAI paper (2025) and agrees with the Nguyen papers (2016).
It also connects somewhat with the Jordanian estimation paper (2018) for cost allocation and
planning required for a project to develop. However, the paper is more about the author’s
personal opinion itself.

Quote or number worth citing
About 20% of the team had started using AI tools, 43% planned to use it and 23% of experts
use it regularly.

Title: Paper 6

Crowdsourcing in Software Development: Empirical Support for Configuring Contests

Author: Stamatia Bibi, Ioannis Zozas, Apostolos Ampatzoglou, Panagiotis G. Sarigiannidis,
George Kalampokis and Ioannis Stamelos (universities in Greece)

Year: 2020

Where: IEEE Access, Vol. 8, article starting at p. 58094.

Type of study: case study

Who they studied: The paper studied coding two contests, and participants were
outsourced freelance developers who competed to win each of the given tasks.

Main Finding
The study found out that crowdsourcing seems to be a better way to get software
development tasks done, even though successful completion depends a lot on task type.
However, AI contests seem to attract more participants, but QA contests finish the fastest.
One concern that the paper showed is that development contests have very low prizes for
the amount of work done; due to this, managers should carefully analyse their goals and pick
the task type.

Engineer vs Manager:
In the paper, both the roles are split into two halves. Managers organise the programme and
decide what to crowdsource, why, when and how. On the other hand, engineers solve the
problem.

Useful for us because:
This paper shows how managers' work affects the results with real numbers.

Connection with other papers
The paper agrees with the Jordanian estimation paper (2018) and connects to the Nguyen
papers (2016). However, it connects loosely to the GenAI paper.

Quote or number worth citing
Design example: 60% of contests were successful across all indicators, like solution, time
and quality.
