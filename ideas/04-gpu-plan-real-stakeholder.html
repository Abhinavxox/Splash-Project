Pay-per-use AI hosting for a real team

We help a real team run their AI model so they stop paying for idle GPUs, without slow or failed responses when traffic spikes.

The problem

[Real team] pays for a GPU that sits idle most of the day. Turning it off saves money but makes users wait or get errors when usage suddenly jumps. How can they pay only for what they use and still stay fast and reliable?

We prove the problem is real with their evidence: their GPU bill, their usage logs, past slowdowns or errors, and an interview with whoever pays or fixes it.

Who is involved

  • Feels the problem: the people using the model (they wait or get errors)
  • Owns the problem: whoever pays the GPU bill (lab lead, founder)
  • Can change it: the team's developer or admin
  • Uses our software: that developer, who deploys the model on our setup

What we research

  1. Where does the money go? Busy hours vs idle hours.
  2. What does usage look like? Steady, bursty, deadline spikes?
  3. How long can users wait before it's a problem?
  4. Which option fits best? We compare smaller GPU, smaller (quantized) model, serverless scale-to-zero, spot instances, batching, and a queue.
  5. We test the top 2–3 options with their real model and measure the trade-offs.

The finding is which option wins for their usage, backed by numbers. We don't pick the answer first.

What we build

A small system, only as much as the findings need: an API that accepts requests, a queue that absorbs spikes, GPU workers that scale down to zero when idle, and a dashboard showing cost, wait time and errors. We deploy it through dev, staging and prod.

No user accounts, no polished frontend, no business model.

How we know it worked

We set targets before building, then run their real workload on our setup for 2–3 weeks and compare it with their old setup.

Budget

About $250–$640 total, using cloud credits first. Hard cap of $1,000/month with alerts and a kill switch. The real team's own spend should go down.

Main risk

We can't find a real team by the end of week 4. Fallback: the same approach on cloud waste in a real account (a lab's or a small company's), which is cheaper and simpler and still measured with real bills.
